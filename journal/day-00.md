# Day 0 – Why I'm Starting Over

This second half of 2026, I decided to transition fully into tech as a medical student.

As someone with way too many interests, picking one skill was hard because every field looked interesting... and honestly, doable. But somehow I landed on AI Automation.

**Because why not?**

There's just something about AI that fascinates me. The way it works. The possibilities. The fact that we're teaching machines to reason through problems. I knew the long-term goal was AI Engineering, but I also knew I had to start somewhere.
So I did my research and gathered every resource I thought would help me learn AI Automation.
I started with theory. I read, watched videos, and tried to understand the basic concepts first. I just wanted to get familiar with the world before jumping into building things.
Eventually, I felt like I had consumed enough theory.
Time for the practicals.

-----------------------

I found a YouTuber running a 30-Day AI Automation Challenge, and I followed it from beginning to end.
By the time I finished, I had built over 20 workflows.
Sounds great, right?
Well... not exactly.
I knew how to build workflows.
But was that really enough?
I had followed the tutorial step by step. Click by click.
I built what he built.
I clicked what he clicked.
And that was it.
The problem was that I had no real understanding of the mechanics behind what I was doing. I could recreate the workflow, but if you asked me why a certain node was there, why a particular option was selected, or why one configuration worked better than another...
I'd probably just stare at you.
But if I'm being honest, that challenge helped me more than I realized.
It introduced me to the world of AI Automation in a way that made it feel less intimidating. Before then, everything looked complex. After 30 days, it started to feel... achievable.
So no, copying the instructor wasn't pointless.
It was actually a necessary stage of learning.
Then I asked myself a question:
Is this the kind of engineer I want to become?
The answer was no.
One thing about me is that I care deeply about doing things well. If I'm going to build systems, I don't just want to know what to click. I want to understand why I'm clicking it. I want to know the problem every node solves, why it was chosen, what alternatives exist, and what would happen if I removed it.
So instead of rushing into more tutorials...
I went all the way back to Day 1.
This time with a completely different strategy.
My Learning System
I opened a new ChatGPT project (and Claude too) called AI Automation.
I fed it everything: where I currently was, how I had learned so far, what my long-term goals were, and most importantly, how I wanted to learn from this point forward.
Then I created a conversation called Learning from Scratch.
Here's the workflow I now follow for every tutorial:
Watch the YouTube video once to understand the overall goal.
Copy the video's transcript into ChatGPT.
Let ChatGPT break down every single concept from first principles.
- Every instruction.
- Every technical term.
- Every button.
- Every configuration.
- Every decision.

Not just what the instructor did.
But why they did it.
Why was this node used?
Why was this option enabled?
Why wasn't that one selected?
What problem is this solving?
Only after I understand all of that do I go back and watch the tutorial again.

The second watch feels completely different because now I'm seeing the reasoning behind every action.
After that, I rebuild the workflow entirely from memory.

No following along.

No pausing every few seconds.

Just me trying to recreate it.

Then comes my favourite part.
I rebuild the exact same workflow...
...but with my own real-world scenario.
Usually something a little more complicated.
That's where I really discover whether I understood the lesson or simply memorized it.
If something breaks, I debug it.

Every error becomes another lesson.
All while constantly discussing my thought process with ChatGPT.
I repeat this process for every single tutorial.
I call it The Meraki Workflow.
It's my attempt to learn AI Automation as deeply as I possibly can.
Maybe one day it'll evolve into something completely different. Maybe I'll realize parts of it don't work and improve them along the way.
But for now, this is how I'm choosing to learn.
Not by rushing to finish tutorials.
Not by collecting certificates.
But by understanding one concept deeply enough that I can explain it, rebuild it, and eventually teach it. 
I don't know where it'll take me.
Maybe six months from now I'll look back and laugh at how little I knew.
I honestly hope I do.
Because that's proof that I kept learning.
This is Day 0.
